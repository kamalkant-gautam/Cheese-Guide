Why It Matters

More Than Just a Topping

Cheese is one of humanity's oldest crafted foods — a product of patience, chemistry, and regional tradition that turns simple milk into thousands of distinct flavors and textures.

Flavor & Texture

Ranges from mild and milky to sharp and pungent, shaped by milk, culture, and time.

Culinary Versatility

Melts, grates, spreads, or crumbles — adapting to almost any dish or cuisine.

Cultural Heritage

Each cheese carries the history, geography, and tradition of the place it comes from.

Nutritional Value

A concentrated source of protein and calcium, valuable as part of a balanced diet.

Craftsmanship

From curdling to aging, cheesemaking blends science with generations of hands-on skill.

Universal Appeal

Enjoyed everywhere — from everyday snacking to the finest dining tables.

The Source

Milk & Origin

Every cheese begins with a milk source, and that milk shapes everything downstream — fat content, flavor intensity, and which cheeses it's suited to become.

Most common

🐄 Cow's Milk

~3.5–4% fat · mild, sweet base

The default milk for the majority of the world's cheeses — mild and versatile, taking well to both fresh and long-aged styles.

  • Mozzarella, Cheddar, Gouda
  • Brie, Camembert, Parmesan
  • Cream Cheese, Monterey Jack
Rich & complex

🐐 Goat's Milk

~4% fat · brighter, tangier

Lower in cholesterol than cow's milk, with a distinct tang; most goat cheeses are eaten fresh or lightly aged.

  • Chèvre (Goat Cheese)
  • Some Feta blends
  • Some Halloumi blends
High fat, savory

🐑 Sheep's Milk

~6–7% fat · dense, nutty, savory

Higher in fat and protein than cow's milk, giving sheep's-milk cheeses their characteristic richness and depth.

  • Feta, Manchego
  • Roquefort
  • Traditional Ricotta
Prized, delicate

🐃 Buffalo Milk

~7–8% fat · silky, milky-sweet

Higher fat content produces an especially creamy, luxurious texture — the gold standard for certain fresh cheeses.

  • Mozzarella di Bufala
  • Paneer (regional)
  • Burrata (occasionally)
From Farm to Wheel

How Cheese Is Made

Nearly every cheese — fresh or aged, soft or hard — follows the same five-stage journey. What changes from one cheese to the next is the milk, the culture, the cutting, and the time.

Fresh cheeses

Skip ripening almost entirely — eaten within days (Mozzarella, Ricotta, Cream Cheese, Paneer).

Bloomy & washed rinds

Ripen from the outside in over 2–6 weeks, with molds or washes actively shaping flavor (Brie, Camembert).

Semi-hard & hard cheeses

Pressed firmer to expel more moisture, then aged for months to years (Gouda, Cheddar, Parmesan).

Blue cheeses

Pierced with needles during aging so air reaches the interior, letting Penicillium mold veins develop throughout.

The Directory

20 Cheeses, A–Z by Family

Mozzarella

🇮🇹 ItalyCow / Buffalo
Texture: Soft, fresh, elastic when melted
Flavor: Mild and milky, with a clean dairy sweetness
Best used in: Pizzas, lasagna, pasta bakes, caprese salad, sandwiches
💡 The buffalo-milk version, mozzarella di bufala, is a protected specialty from the Campania region near Naples.

Burrata

🇮🇹 ItalyCow
Texture: Mozzarella shell filled with cream and soft curd
Flavor: Rich, luscious, buttery
Best used in: Antipasti platters, caprese-style starters, drizzled with olive oil and torn into salads
💡 Created in Puglia as a way to use up mozzarella scraps and cream — nothing went to waste.

Ricotta

🇮🇹 ItalyCow / Sheep Whey
Texture: Light, grainy-soft, spoonable
Flavor: Mildly sweet, delicate
Best used in: Lasagna, ravioli filling, cannoli, baked cheesecakes, desserts
💡 The name means "recooked" — it's traditionally made from the whey left over after making other cheeses.

Feta

🇬🇷 GreeceSheep / Goat
Texture: Firm yet crumbly, brined
Flavor: Salty and tangy
Best used in: Greek salad, spanakopita, Mediterranean mezze, appetizer platters
💡 Under EU law, only cheese made in Greece from sheep and goat milk can be labeled "Feta."

Paneer

🇮🇳 IndiaCow / Buffalo
Texture: Firm, does not melt when cooked
Flavor: Neutral, milky
Best used in: Paneer tikka, palak paneer, curries, kebabs — a banquet staple
💡 Made by curdling milk with an acid (lemon juice or vinegar) rather than rennet, and pressing out the whey.

Cream Cheese

🇺🇸 USACow
Texture: Soft, smooth, spreadable
Flavor: Mild and creamy
Best used in: Cheesecakes, bagels, canapés, frostings, dips
💡 Invented in New York in 1872; the Philadelphia brand made it a household name worldwide.

Mascarpone

🇮🇹 ItalyCow
Texture: Ultra-smooth, thick like whipped cream
Flavor: Rich and buttery
Best used in: Tiramisu, dessert fillings, pasta sauces
💡 Technically not a true cheese — it's made by curdling cream with acid, not rennet.

Goat Cheese (Chèvre)

🇫🇷 FranceGoat
Texture: Creamy fresh log to firmer aged wheel
Flavor: Tangy, earthy
Best used in: Salads, tarts, crostini, gourmet appetizers, fine dining plates
💡 Ranges widely by age — a fresh log tastes nothing like a rind-aged crottin.

Brie

🇫🇷 FranceCow
Texture: Soft with a bloomy white edible rind
Flavor: Buttery, mild, slightly earthy
Best used in: Cheese platters, baked brie, fine dining courses
💡 Known as the "Queen of Cheeses" — it hails from the Brie region just east of Paris.

Camembert

🇫🇷 FranceCow
Texture: Soft, bloomy rind, runnier when ripe
Flavor: Rich, earthy, more pungent than Brie
Best used in: Baked whole, cheese boards, paired with crusty bread
💡 Normandy's answer to Brie — smaller wheels, a stronger character.

Halloumi

🇨🇾 CyprusSheep / Goat
Texture: Semi-hard, brined, springy and squeaky
Flavor: Salty, tangy
Best used in: Grilling, pan-frying, Mediterranean mezze boards, salads
💡 Its unusually high melting point means it holds its shape beautifully on a hot grill.

Gouda

🇳🇱 NetherlandsCow
Texture: Smooth, semi-hard
Flavor: Nutty, slightly sweet, caramel notes with age
Best used in: Sandwiches, cheese platters, breakfast buffets
💡 Named after the Dutch city of Gouda, historically the market town where the cheese was traded.

Swiss (Emmental)

🇨🇭 SwitzerlandCow
Texture: Semi-hard with characteristic large holes ("eyes")
Flavor: Mild, nutty
Best used in: Sandwiches, burgers, fondue, gratins
💡 The holes form from carbon dioxide bubbles released by bacteria during fermentation.

Gruyère

🇨🇭 SwitzerlandCow
Texture: Dense, semi-hard to firm
Flavor: Earthy, nutty, complex — more intense with age
Best used in: Fondue, French onion soup, gratins, quiche
💡 A protected AOC cheese and a cornerstone ingredient of classic French cuisine.

Provolone

🇮🇹 ItalyCow
Texture: Semi-hard, smooth
Flavor: Mild ("dolce") to sharp ("piccante"), depending on age
Best used in: Paninis, pizzas, grilled sandwiches
💡 Sold in two classic styles — dolce (mild, aged 2–3 months) and piccante (sharp, aged much longer).

Monterey Jack

🇺🇸 USACow
Texture: Semi-hard, melts easily
Flavor: Mild and buttery
Best used in: Mexican dishes, nachos, burgers, casseroles
💡 Developed in Monterey, California, in the 18th century by Franciscan missionaries.

Manchego

🇪🇸 SpainSheep
Texture: Firm, semi-hard to hard with a distinctive zigzag rind pattern
Flavor: Nutty, tangy, buttery
Best used in: Tapas boards, charcuterie, paired with membrillo (quince paste)
💡 Must come from Manchega sheep raised in Spain's La Mancha region to carry the name legally.

Cheddar

🇬🇧 EnglandCow
Texture: Semi-hard to hard, white or orange
Flavor: Sharp and rich, intensifying with age
Best used in: Burgers, sandwiches, omelets, sauces
💡 Named after the village of Cheddar in Somerset, England, where it was first made in local caves.

Parmesan (Parmigiano-Reggiano)

🇮🇹 ItalyCow
Texture: Hard, granular, aged
Flavor: Strong, nutty, deeply umami
Best used in: Grated over pasta, risotto, soups, and salads
💡 Aged a minimum of 12 months — premium wheels are often aged 24 to 36+ months.

Blue Cheese Family

🇫🇷🇮🇹🇬🇧 France / Italy / EnglandSheep / Cow
Texture: Crumbly to creamy, veined with mold
Flavor: Sharp, tangy, pungent
Best used in: Salads, dressings, steak sauces, gourmet cheese boards
Key variants: Roquefort (France, sheep), Gorgonzola (Italy, cow), Stilton (England, cow)
💡 The blue veins come from Penicillium mold cultures deliberately introduced during aging.
At A Glance

The Texture Spectrum

Cheeses fall along a spectrum from fresh and unaged to hard and long-matured — a useful mental map when building a cheese board or planning a menu.

Speak the Language

A Glossary of Cheese Terms

Affinage
The controlled aging and ripening process that develops a cheese's final flavor and texture.
Bloomy Rind
A soft, white, edible mold rind (e.g. Brie, Camembert) that ripens the cheese from the outside in.
Brine
A salt-water solution used to wash, preserve, or firm certain cheeses such as Feta and Halloumi.
Casein
The primary milk protein that coagulates to form curd during cheesemaking.
Curd
The solid part of milk that separates from the whey once it coagulates — the raw material of all cheese.
Culture (Starter)
Live bacteria added to milk to begin fermentation and develop flavor.
Emmental Eyes
The characteristic large holes in Swiss-style cheeses, formed by trapped carbon dioxide gas.
Mold-Ripened
Cheese that develops flavor and texture through the action of introduced mold cultures, inside or on the rind.
Pasteurization
Heat-treating milk to eliminate harmful bacteria before cheesemaking.
PDO / AOC
Protected designation labels (Protected Designation of Origin / Appellation d'Origine Contrôlée) that restrict a cheese name to a specific region and method.
Raw Milk
Unpasteurized milk, sometimes used for artisanal cheeses to preserve natural flavor complexity.
Rennet
An enzyme (animal, microbial, or plant-based) used to coagulate milk into curds.
Rind
The cheese's outer layer, which may be edible (bloomy, washed) or inedible (waxed, natural).
Ripening
The aging period during which flavor, aroma, and texture continue to develop after the cheese is formed.
Semi-Soft
A texture category between fresh and semi-hard — pliable, moderate moisture, easy-melting.
Terroir
The combined effect of a region's soil, climate, and pasture on a cheese's character.
Veining
The blue-green marbling in blue cheeses, produced by Penicillium mold cultures.
Washed Rind
A rind regularly bathed in brine, wine, or spirits during aging, producing a pungent aroma and orange hue.
Wheel
The traditional cylindrical form in which many cheeses are aged and sold.
Whey
The liquid left behind after curds form — the base ingredient for cheeses like Ricotta.
Zigzag Rind
A decorative rind pattern pressed into certain hard cheeses, such as Manchego, from traditional esparto grass molds.
